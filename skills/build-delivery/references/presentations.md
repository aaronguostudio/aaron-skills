# Presentations

Read when building or revising a deck. Use available presentation tools and their
specialist instructions for authoring, rendering and inspection. Follow the
requested medium; do not substitute a web page for an editable PowerPoint file.

## Fit the audience and occasion

Use the brief to identify what the audience should understand, decide or do.
Distinguish a live talk, a deck someone reads alone and a deck another person
must present. They can need different density, transitions and speaker notes.
Preserve the user's argument, duration, terminology and supplied template.

Build a narrative that earns its conclusion with relevant evidence. Make slide
titles and visuals carry the point. Choose tables, diagrams, images or charts
for their explanatory purpose; do not force every idea into the same card grid.
Label fictional or illustrative material, and preserve the status of proposed
decisions and forecasts. Never fill a missing source with invented evidence.

## Resolve style through real content

Reuse accepted masters, brand assets and representative slides. When direction
is uncertain, create a small sample containing the difficult content, such as a
dense comparison or important chart, rather than only a decorative cover.
Apply feedback to the whole deck's hierarchy, density and tone where relevant.
Do not require another approval for an established style or trivial edit.

## Inspect what the audience receives

Render the actual deck and inspect every changed slide and affected neighbors;
for a new deck, inspect the complete result. Check clipping, line breaks,
contrast, font substitution, chart labels and readability at the intended viewing
size. Verify figures and sources separately from layout. Check slide order,
cross-references and notes after content changes.

When both an editable file and a PDF are requested, inspect both: the export
can differ from the authoring view. Keep text/charts editable where required
rather than flattening the deck into images. Check asset and font dependencies,
links and any promised animation or media in a suitable viewer; disclose what
cannot be exercised. A generated preview is not evidence of PowerPoint playback.

Hand over the requested file and supporting material needed by the presenter.
Include sources, notes or usage instructions where useful, without automatically
adding a separate report. State any unresolved data or compatibility limit.
