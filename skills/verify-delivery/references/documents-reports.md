# Documents and reports

Read for written deliverables, analytical reports and their exports. Use the
relevant document/data tools and specialist verification when available.

## Check completeness against the question

Compare the actual contents with what the reader needs to understand, decide or
do and with any explicit deliverable requirements. Inspect the argument, relevant
exceptions, instructions, conclusions and next actions. Do not equate a table of
contents or a filled template with substantive coverage. Do not require sections
that would add no value to the requested document.

Keep evidence, interpretation, recommendation and decision status distinguishable.
Cross-check important statements across prose, tables, diagrams, examples and
appendices. A request for review does not alone establish an approval condition.
When a source is missing or contradictory, preserve that uncertainty instead of
writing a plausible fact to complete the narrative.

## Verify facts and quantitative reasoning

Trace material claims to the relevant supplied or authorized source. Check dates,
names, quotations and source accessibility where they affect interpretation.
Verify current external claims from an authoritative source when needed; record
the source date or other relevant freshness limit. A citation's presence does
not prove it supports the sentence.

For an analytical report, check the data's applicable population, time period,
grain, units, missingness and method. Recompute important totals, percentages or
comparisons independently of the rendered text when source data is available.
Confirm charts, tables and conclusions use the same definitions and relevant
filters. Do not silently treat missing data as zero, infer causation from an
association or generalize a partial sample to the whole population.

If the source data or method is unavailable, identify which conclusions cannot
be substantiated. Distinguish verified arithmetic from an unverified business
definition; a sum can be correct for the wrong population.

## Check the reading and editing result

Inspect the target reading format. For a new document, inspect all rendered
pages; for revisions, include changed pages and affected pagination. Check tables,
headings, page breaks, footnotes, links, figure references and readable text.
Open/check editable source and requested exports separately. Preserve requested
tracked changes, comments and meaningful structure.

Recheck dependent claims, calculations and exports after a content repair.
Keep the final handoff concise: what is supported, what was corrected and what
the reader still needs to resolve. A complete-looking report is not evidence
that the underlying data or recommendation is sound.
